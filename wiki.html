01 · WHY

The idea

Games are the medium. Mathematics is the learning. Human values are the background.

Maths for All is a free learning space where children meet mathematics through play, making, noticing, experimenting and trying again.

We are not trying to remove mathematical challenge. We are trying to remove unnecessary barriers around it.

Enter easily. The first action should make sense without a long lesson.
Explore freely. A learner should have room to try, change an idea and try again.
Notice something. The activity should create a reason to think mathematically.
Leave with more agency. The child should feel that they can investigate mathematics themselves.
02 · HOW IT SHOULD FEEL

Our learning philosophy

🎈 Play before explanation Let the learner encounter the idea through an action before giving the formal definition.
🌱 Mistakes are information A wrong answer or surprising result opens another path instead of closing the door.
🔎 Curiosity is valuable “What happens if I change this?” is already mathematical thinking.
🕰️ Patience matters Not every idea becomes clear immediately. Looking again is part of learning.
💪 Perseverance matters Trying another way should feel normal rather than like punishment.
⚖️ Values live inside the game Fairness, honesty, planning and responsibility should emerge from what the learner does.

    04 · EVERYDAY MATHEMATICS

    Little Shop: the fruit counter 🍎

    Little Shop is an open-ended shop-counter activity. Customers keep arriving, and the learner can continue for as long as they like. There are no levels, countdowns or score comparisons.

    🧺 Fill the basket Read the customer's fruit order, choose the right items and quantities, and check the basket before billing.
    🧮 Make the bill Match the order and work with the total using simulated money.
    💵 Count the payment Decide whether the customer paid too little, the exact amount or more than the bill.
    🪙 Give the change When the customer pays extra, calculate the difference and return it with the on-screen notes and coins.

    Designed for young learners

    The counter uses a wide shelf on desktop screens so more fruit choices can fit without crowding the rest of the activity. Fruit names, prices, order details and feedback use larger, clearer text. The shelf adapts to smaller screens by showing fewer columns instead of squeezing the labels. Buttons remain large enough to tap comfortably, while compact spacing helps limit unnecessary scrolling.

    The shelf currently contains 22 fruit choices. Money is simulated; calculations use integer paise internally. The activity does not collect personal information or save a learner profile. Supervised learner and educator review is still needed.

    05 · FOR EVERYONE

    Accessibility

    Accessibility is part of the experience, not a final decoration pass. Clear design helps children with disabilities and makes the games easier for everyone.

    Large controls Readable text Visible focus Keyboard friendly Clear grouping Reduced motion Responsive layouts Repeat freely

    The project tries not to make important information depend only on colour, tiny targets, precise pointer movements or speed.

    Accessibility is not a special version of the game. It is part of the game we are building.

    06 · THE CHILD'S EXPERIENCE

    What we want children to feel

    “I can try.” The first interaction should feel approachable.
    “I can change my mind.” A prediction is allowed to be wrong.
    “I can find out.” Questions should lead to something the learner can investigate.
    “I can try another way.” A failed attempt should open another possibility.
    “I noticed something.” Observation is a mathematical skill.
    “I understand why.” The goal is understanding, not merely producing an answer.
    07 · FOR ADULTS

    For parents, teachers and facilitators

    Adults do not need to turn every game into a formal lesson. Sometimes the best support is one good question.

    What do you notice? Why did you choose that? What surprised you? What if we changed this? Would you try again? Is there another way?

    Give children room to explain their own reasoning. A child's explanation can reveal much more than a correct button press.

    Using the games together

    Shape Architect Children can discuss where a piece belongs and what happens when it is rotated.
    Sudoku Ask why a number belongs somewhere rather than asking only for the answer.
    Arithmetic Ask children to explain different ways they reached the same answer.
    08 · TRUST

    Privacy and trust

    Playing a game should not automatically mean participating in research. Research participation is explicitly opt-in.

    Choice matters A learner or responsible user should be able to understand whether research participation is being requested.
    Privacy information stays accessible People should not have to create an account just to understand the privacy boundary.
    Personal information is not a prize The project does not treat children's information as something to collect simply because it can.
    Research has a boundary The published privacy information explains what research participation means and what lies outside it.
    09 · RESPONSIBILITY

    Responsible use of AI

    AI tools may help the people building Maths for All with ideas, writing, programming, debugging, testing, documentation and review.

    AI is not treated as the final authority for mathematical correctness, accessibility, educational quality, privacy or whether an activity is genuinely useful.

    People remain responsible for the final experience.

    13 · OUR PROMISE

    A small project with a big reason

    Maths for All does not need to solve every problem in education.

    It can still try to make one corner of learning a little kinder.

    We want mathematics to feel less like a gate deciding who belongs and more like a place where everyone can enter, look around, ask questions and make something.

    The website is the artifact. The child is the reason.
